# Introduction

The rapid development of generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) platforms has presented new opportunities for multimodal composing in first year composition multimodal composition classrooms. As a teacher of writing and digital media myself, I spent much of

2023 and 2024 experimenting with GenAI tools such as ChatGPT and DALL.E because I was so excited to bring them to my students. Growing interest among teachers and scholars in GenAI has led to an emerging body of research at the intersection of GenAI and multimodal composition. Teachers and educators have called for the use of various GenAI tools to assist students’ writing projects, including, among others, Open AI’s ChatGPT (Barrot; Yan; Vetter et al. “Towards”) and DALL.E (Jiang “When”; Kang and Yi; Liu et al.). This chapter aims to demonstrate how students can effectively leverage GenAI tools to enhance their digital storytelling practices. Scholars and educators in digital storytelling have explored the affordances of platforms such as Canva (Pani) and Vyond (Jiang “Emotions”) for cultivating students’ digital literacy.

Compared to traditional multimodal composition, AI-assisted composing opens new avenues for storytelling. Digital storytelling emerged from the community arts movement in the 1990s as a short, narrated film (Lambert) produced by media production amateurs. Compared to traditional storytelling, digital stories further harness students’ technological proficiency to generate creative forms of expression, such as video creation and multimedia narratives. AI-assisted video creators such as Canva are ideal for beginner students who are just starting to explore how to integrate multimodal elements into their digital storytelling projects. Canva provides interfaces that facilitate the creation and editing of digital stories through GenAI-powered image and video generation. Vyond, a more specialized animation design platform, offers more advanced tools for customizing cartoon characters, scenes, and animations, including text-to-video features that convert scripts directly into visual stories.

As students in higher education, you will discover how AI-powered platforms such as Canva and Vyond can enhance your storytelling abilities and help you create stories that are engaging and impactful (Jiang “Emotions”). Traditional multimodal composition combines various modes like text, visuals, audio, and video to communicate messages effectively (Gagich 67; Tham and Jiang). Building on your understanding of multimodal composition, GenAI tools allows you to creatively express key ideas, engage deeply with audiences, and navigate new forms of digital literacy through storytelling.

# What Is Digital Storytelling 2.0?

Digital storytelling originally emerged from community arts initiatives as short, personal films that blend narration with visual media (Lambert). Digital storytelling is generally defined as a short, narrated film or media presentation, often 2-3 minutes long, that is created by individuals who are typically not professional media producers. These stories combine various digital elements such as graphics, audio, video clips, music, photographs, and voice-overs to tell a story, which are often related to the storytellers’ personal experiences. Digital stories encompass the steps of compiling photos and digital assets, creating a script of one’s life stories, planning out visuals through storyboarding, and producing short films about two to three minutes in length. The combined uses of multiple modes of communication (Kendrick et al.) can help create short digital stories that are engaging and accessible.

Over time, the format of digital storytelling has expanded beyond simple personal narratives to include diverse, culturally sensitive storytelling techniques that engage broader communities and address social justice issues. Digital stories can not only simplify the storytelling process but also have the potential to enhance learner autonomy and encourage deeper emotional and cognitive engagement (Lazareva and Cruz-Martinez). For instance, digital stories could function as a healing method (Jiang; Kendrick et al.; Lewis Ellison and Wang) of counter-storytelling for refugee learners and provide opportunities for international students to navigate a myriad of feelings, including shame. Moreover, these tools empower storytellers from diverse linguistic and cultural backgrounds (Kendrick et al.), thereby providing the platforms for these learners to confront social inequalities through creative expression.

Digital Storytelling 2.0, or storytelling in the age of AI, further marks a shift where GenAI technologies significantly influence the storytelling process. AI-generated content such as images, animations, and texts allow storytellers to overcome technical limitations and focus more closely on creativity and narrative development (Jiang). As you explore Digital Storytelling 2.0, reflect on how leveraging GenAI might reshape your understanding of storytelling and your ability to engage audiences effectively.

# Why Use GenAI for Digital Storytelling?

GenAI, through lowering the technical barriers for digital creativity, allows you to explore and implement your multimedia design elements without extensive prior experience (Jiang “When”). Instead of feeling constrained by the advanced features in video creation platforms, you can spend more time on developing engaging narratives and clear rhetorical objectives, which will help enhance your storytelling quality and effectiveness.

Additionally, leveraging GenAI tools can prepare you effectively for contemporary professional environments. As digital literacy and multimedia skills become increasingly valued across the disciplines, getting familiar with GenAI tools will help make you a competitive multimodal content creator in the job market. Such an experience will help prepare you for roles in diverse fields including media production, marketing, education, and communication.

Moreover, integrating GenAI tools into your storytelling projects bridges your everyday digital practices with academic and professional composition tasks. These technologies encourage collaborative, interactive, and innovative approaches. GenAI tools will help align your personal digital competencies with formal academic expectations and enhance your overall learning experience. For instance, GenAI can act as a scaffolding tool that provides support for navigating both personal tasks, such as crafting social media posts or engaging in creative projects, and academic tasks, including academic writing and multimodal design work.

# Key GenAI Tools for Storytelling

Since both novice and experienced students can benefit from GenAI-assisted design platforms, this chapter will focus on providing guidance on how to use two GenAI-powered platforms to facilitate storytelling: Canva [www.canva.com] and Vyond [www.vyond.com]. Canva’s AI-powered design features simplify the creation of visually appealing content without the need for advanced technical skills. In comparison, Vyond is a more advanced platform suitable for students with prior experience in digital media and design. As a cloud-based storytelling platform, Vyond encompasses professional features for students to manually create animated videos, while also including text-to-video features available through Vyond Go. This platform allows you to design unique cartoon characters, animations, and detailed scenes. The text-to-video storytelling feature provided by Vyond Go further streamlines the video composing process by converting your written narratives directly into short video clips that can further undergo revisions and manual editing according to your rhetorical purposes.

# Step-by-Step Guide to Creating Digital Stories with GenAI

Now that you’ve learned why and how GenAI tools like Canva and Vyond can enhance your storytelling, this section will take you through a detailed, step-by-step process of creating your own engaging digital story. Following these clear steps will facilitate your storytelling process and allow you to produce effective narratives that resonate with your intended audience.

# Step 1: Determine Your Rhetorical Situation

Before starting your project, it’s crucial to clearly identify your rhetorical situation, which includes your “purpose,” “audience,” the “author,” the “genre,” and the “medium” (Gagich 75-76). Defining your rhetorical situation will help make sure that your digital story stays focused and impactful. The first step for analyzing your rhetorical situation involves understanding the purpose of your design. You should consider what you want to communicate with your digital story.

In terms of analyzing the audience, you should consider both the intended audience, i.e., those you are specifically targeting with your key message and the unintentional audience, i.e., those who might encounter your text by chance. When considering your intended audience, you should think about their beliefs, values, and demographics. Gagich suggests approaching the audience using the concept of “discourse communities,” or the groups of people who share a common interest and use the same language to talk and write about that interest.

Furthermore, as the author, you need to consider your relationship to both the message and the audience. This process includes recognizing any explicit (obvious) and/or implicit (not obvious) biases you might bring to your message and how these could affect it and your audience. You should also think about your familiarity with the intended audience and how that might influence your message. It would also be beneficial to determine the type of text you want to create (Gagich). After brainstorming ideas for the rhetorical situation, including the purpose, audience, author, genre, and medium, it’s time to find other texts representative of your topic and genre to analyze.

Finally, the medium refers to where you plan to distribute your digital story, or the way the story is distributed in relation to your target audience. By carefully considering these five components, you can effectively analyze your rhetorical situation before beginning to compose your digital story. Activity: Take five minutes to write down a brief description of your intended audience. Ask yourself: Who are they? Why should they care about your story? Then, jot down the central objective of your narrative: What main idea or emotional response do you want to communicate?

# Step 2: Gather and Organize Digital Assets

Your story’s visual and audio elements are crucial for engaging your viewers. These design elements can include images, video clips, audio tracks, animations, and other multimedia assets. GenAI tools can generate some materials for you, but supplementing AI-generated content with manually collected digital assets will enhance your autonomy (Jiang “When”) as a digital storyteller.

The first step in gathering digital assets is to conduct content research, which includes the information that supports your message. The extent of the research required will depend on the specific assignment. Some instructors may expect scholarly research, while others may not. Therefore, it is crucial to read the assignment closely and then conduct the necessary research. Gagich provides an example of a student website and videos about the similarities between American music and K-pop, where the content research included both a scholarly article and a popular article.

In addition to textual information, you should also gather images, sounds, videos, animations, memes, among others, that you wish to incorporate into your multimodal text. When using existing materials, it is important to be aware of open licenses and Creative Commons licenses. These licenses allow creators to use copyrighted work under specific conditions. There are various websites where you can find openly licensed work, such as CC Search, Pexels, Unsplash, Freesound, and Wikimedia Commons. You can also use filters on platforms like Flickr or Google Images to locate openly licensed content.

Last but not least, you may consider using GenAI to create digital assets. GenAI tools such as OpenAI’s ChatGPT, DALL.E, and Sora provide additional opportunities for you to generate image and video content. Furthermore, you can benefit from audio generators such as ElevenLabs and Speechify. However, it is important to cite and attribute GenAI-created content in your final digital story (See “Citing and Acknowledging GenAI Content” in this chapter).

Activity: Explore online repositories offering openly licensed content, such as Unsplash (photos), Pexels (videos), or Freesound (audio). Explore GenAI platforms that allow you to generate multimodal content, such as DALL.E (images), Sora (videos), and ElevenLabs (audios). Bookmark or download at least three assets relevant to your story’s theme and message.

# Step 3: Using GenAI Tools

Once you have your assets, it’s time to familiarize yourself with the GenAI tools you’ve selected. For beginners, Canva’s user-friendly interface simplifies the integration of AI-generated visuals. For students more comfortable with digital media, Vyond offers specific design features like customized animations and character creations.

Canva allows you to upload your chosen assets easily and integrate GenAI-generated visuals, videos, and design elements. You may experiment with Canva’s built-in AI tools to generate backgrounds, graphics, or short video clips (Figure 1). Canva provides an accessible and intuitive platform ideal for beginners engaging in digital storytelling. For instance, with the use of textual prompts, you can generate short video clips that are typically around five seconds in length. These AI-generated videos can quickly align visuals with your narrative ideas and facilitate your initial video creation.

Although Canva allows video regeneration if the initial results are unsatisfactory, there is limited capability for fine-tuning the details of the generated scenes directly using the GenAI function. However, Canva compensates for this limitation with its user-friendly built-in video editor. Through this editor, you can easily supplement the AI-generated clips with additional scenes, incorporate smooth transitions, and enhance your story with appropriate sound effects and background music. In this way, you can start building a cohesive and engaging story that draw on a combination of generated videos and the digital assets you’ve collected from license-free websites.

Tips for embedding and editing AI-generated content:

- Supplement AI-generated content with your digital assets, such as manually collected/edited images and customized characters.
- Edit AI-generated scenes and props to enhance the clarity of your video and build a smoother transition between the scenes.
- Adjust AI-generated scripts and voiceovers to align the video with your narrative’s purposes.

# Step 4: Storyboarding and Scriptwriting

Organizing your narrative visually and textually can streamline your video production process. Storyboarding helps map out each scene and provide a visual sequence for your digital story (Kendrick et al. 963), which makes the structure of your story look more tangible and manageable. Meanwhile, incorporating scripts within your storyboard will help you map your dialogue, narration, and textual content in a clear and concise manner.

To assist you in this process, I’ve provided a storyboard template (Figure 3) designed specifically to help you map out each scene in your digital story comprehensively. This template allows you to define character or object movements, script dialogues or narrations, and add music or sound effects to each scene. You have the option to print the template and use traditional pen and paper to sketch your scenes, or alternatively, you may use your digital tablet to draw and arrange your storyboard digitally. It is important to remember that the goal here is not to produce highly detailed or polished artworks, but rather to visualize and concretize your creative ideas before proceeding to the actual video composing. This approach helps make sure that you build a strong foundation for storytelling during the video composing stage.

# Step 5: Producing and Revising Your Digital Story

Finally, it’s time to put all your assets, AI-generated content, storyboard, and script together into a coherent digital story. After using your selected GenAI platform (Canva or Vyond) to assemble your narrative, you may review your project to ensure the narrative flow remains consistent, solicit feedback from peers or your instructor and refine your project based on their constructive feedback, and create clear, engaging, and rhetorically effective stories that resonates with your intended audience (Jiang, “Emotions” 3; Lazareva and Cruz-Martinez 384). Once you have created your initial video, the revision and peer-review processes are essential for enhancing the clarity of your digital storytelling. Revising your work involves carefully reviewing your digital story and making the necessary adjustments to enhance your narrative effectiveness, audience engagement, and story accessibility.

During the peer-review process, it can be beneficial to consider specific evaluative questions to offer targeted and constructive feedback. Here are several questions you might consider when assessing your peers’ digital stories:

- Multimodal Engagement: How effectively do the visuals, colors, and imagery enhance the key message in the story? How well does the video evoke emotions and connect with the audience’s needs or values?
- Technical Impact: What impact do the video-making techniques (such as cuts, scene transitions, and camera angles) have on the overall quality and flow of the video?
- Audio Contribution: How effectively do the sound effects, voice-overs, and background music contribute to the video’s message and emotional appeal? How well does the background music specifically support the video’s tone and key message?
- Accessibility Considerations: How does the video ensure accessibility (through captions, text, narration) for a broad audience?
- Pacing and Audience Accommodation: How appropriate is the pacing of the video, and how does it accommodate diverse viewers’ needs and preferences?

Addressing these questions during peer reviews will help identify strengths and areas for improvement and allow you to refine your digital stories into more polished and impactful narratives. The revision stage, guided by peer feedback, is crucial in transforming your initial production into an effective digital storytelling piece.

# Citing and Acknowledging GenAI Content

As you’ve incorporated AI-generated materials into your digital storytelling projects, understanding how to ethically acknowledge and cite these materials becomes increasingly important (Vetter et al. “Towards”; Yan). Ethical practices help you uphold academic integrity and respect intellectual property rights.

Using GenAI platforms opens exciting opportunities for enhancing your creativity and productivity. However, it is vital to address the ethical issues related to GenAI yse, including concerns around authorship, originality, and transparency (Jiang et al.; Vetter et al. “Towards”). Being transparent about your use of GenAI helps your audience understand the specific roles these tools played in your creative process.

How to Appropriately Cite AI-generated Materials? According to Gagich, clearly indicating where you have retrieved multimodal content is important in both academic and professional settings (80). While citation formats for AI-generated materials are still emerging, generally, you should clearly specify the GenAI platform used as well as the type of content generated.

Here are examples of how you might cite AI-generated materials:

> AI-generated image citation example: “My Language Journey.” Generated by Canva AI, 12 Jan. 2025, www.canva.com.

> AI-generated video sequence citation example: “Language Journey Animation.” Generated by Vyond Go AI, 15 Feb. 2025, www.vyond.com.

Best practices for acknowledging GenAI content:

- Always mention the AI tool used directly in your project attribution or description.
- Clearly distinguish AI-generated content from your original creative work and openly licensed third-party content.
- Include specific dates when AI-generated materials were created if available, as GenAI-generated outputs can change over time.

Following these guidelines will help you responsibly and ethically engage with the innovative affordances of GenAI in your digital storytelling projects.

# Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

While GenAI tools offer substantial benefits, they also come with several challenges. One major pitfall is students’ over-reliance on automation (Vetter et al. “Towards”). Students might rely too heavily on AI-generated content, which may potentially compromise their own sense of creativity and critical thinking skills.

Additionally, ethical issues, such as biases in AI-generated content and intellectual property concerns, require careful consideration (Vetter et al. “An Endangered”; Vetter et al. “Towards”). To mitigate these issues, you should consider the following: 1) critically evaluate AI-generated materials for biases and inaccuracies; 2) always acknowledge the use of AI-generated content in your digital stories; and 3) regularly review and revise AI-generated content to ensure it aligns with your intended narrative and ethical standards.

Maintaining a balance between automation and personal creative input is key to leveraging GenAI tools effectively, while also allowing you to exercise your own sense of control over the GenAI-created multimodal content.
