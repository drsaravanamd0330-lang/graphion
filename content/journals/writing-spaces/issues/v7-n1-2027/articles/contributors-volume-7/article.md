Jocelyn Ahlers is Professor of Linguistics at California State University, San Marcos in the Linguistics and Liberal Studies Department. She is a linguistic anthropologist who teaches courses including Linguistic Anthropology and Language and Gender. Much of her research focuses on language ideology and the role of language in the production of identity.

Jun Akiyoshi is Assistant Teaching Professor of English at Pennsylvania State University. His research interests include writing teacher identities and the pedagogical applications of autobiographical narratives in college composition courses.

Cristian Aquino-Sterling is Associate Professor of Bi-/Multilingual Education at Texas Tech University. His newly expanded line of research pertains to philosophical approaches to research in bi-/multilingual education and bilingual teacher education in international and comparative perspectives. He is co-founder and director of the International and Comparative Bi-/Multilingual Education Research Group (ICBERG): www.icberg.org.

Nicoleta Bateman is Professor of Linguistics at California State University San Marcos. Her research interests include heritage language acquisition, phonetics and phonology, and the application of linguistics in K-12 education.

Meaghan Brewer is an associate professor at Pace University (NYC), where she teaches courses in writing studies and linguistics and directs the composition program. Much of her work explores the concept of disciplinarity, including what defines a discipline and how disciplines like writing studies and linguistics can work together.

Elizabeth Catchmark (she/her) is the Director of the Writing Center and Assistant Teaching Professor at Georgetown University. Her areas of expertise include linguistic justice, language diversity, and prison education. She also teaches a course on the horror genre, just for fun.

Kaitlin Clinnin is an associate professor of Writing & Rhetoric in the English department at the University of Nevada Las Vegas. Clinnin’s research focuses on trauma in writing programs and classrooms. Her current project is a monograph on mass gun violence memorials in the United States.

Aidan Rose DiCarlo is a recent graduate from Lafayette College where she studied English with a concentration in writing and women’s, gender and sexuality studies.

Kristen di Gennaro is an associate professor at Pace University’s New York City campus. As an applied sociolinguist, her teaching and research focus on real-world issues related to language, such as fairness in writing assessment, critical language awareness in writing pedagogy, and the (in)effectiveness of grammar correction.

Gitte Frandsen is Teaching Assistant Professor at Marquette University where she teaches courses in rhetoric and sociolinguistics. Her research interests focus on cultural rhetorics and translingual, transnational, and translation studies. Her individual and collaborative works have been published in *College Composition and Communication, Composition Studies*, and *College English* (forthcoming).

Bridget Gelms (she/her) is Associate Professor at San Francisco State University, where she teaches in the professional writing program. Her research centers on professionalism as well as digital and social media rhetoric. She is published in *Technical Communication Quarterly,* *Computers and Composition, Composition Forum*, and various edited collections and other journals.

Keith Harms is an assistant professor at the University of the Fraser Valley. He has been teaching writing for fifteen years. His research and practice are situated at the intersection of decolonizing and Indigenizing writing assessment and curriculum.

As Associate Professor of English at Angelo State University, Mellisa Huffman coordinates first-year composition and teaches FYC, advanced writing for majors, technical and business writing, and graduate-level composition theories courses. Her research interests include pop cultural resistance to generative AI, hermeneutics of peer review, and writing class size.

Alex Jennings’s work focuses on the writing processes of neurodivergent students, particularly those with ADHD. Alex loves incorporating digital and multimodal methods into pedagogy and has had the pleasure of teaching composing digital media and professional uses of social media, in addition to the seminar in composition, at the University of Pittsburgh.

Jialei Jiang is a teaching assistant professor of composition at the University of Pittsburgh. She is interested in studying critical AI literacy. Her co-edited collection is titled *Designing for Social Justice: Community-engaged Approaches in Technical and Professional Communication* (Routledge, 2025). Her works have appeared in *College Composition* *and Communication, Computers and Composition, Technical Communication Quarterly*, and edited collections.

Cynthia Johnson (she/her) is an assistant professor and the director of composition at Missouri State University. Her research centers on composition and professional writing pedagogies and can be found in *Technical Communication Quarterly, Computers and Composition, The* *Writing Center Journal*, and edited collections.

Benjamin Keating is Assistant Director of Curricular Innovation at Boston University’s Institute for Excellence in Teaching & Learning. He holds a PhD from University of Michigan (English and Education). In addition to collaborative learning and peer feedback, his interests include inclusive pedagogy and educational development for college instructors.

Gabrielle Isabel Kelenyi is Assistant Professor of English and assistant director of the College Writing Program at Lafayette College. She teaches, studies, and practices community-engaged writing research, antiracism and activism in writing, and composition pedagogy. Kelenyi is a co-editor of “Coda: Community Writing and Creative Work” in *Community Literacy Journal*.

Carolyne M. King’s research focuses upon reading theory and pedagogy, working to encourage greater attention to how reading is as much material as it is cognitive. She is currently an assistant professor at Old Dominion University, and also the Director of WAC/WID and STEM initiatives for the writing center.

Roberto S. Leon is an assistant professor at Georgia College and State University, where he coordinates professional development for graduate teaching assistants and teaches courses in first-year composition, professional writing, and composition pedagogy. His research interests include history of rhetoric, comparative rhetoric, rhetorical education, and writing pedagogy education.

Kelli Lycke is a visiting assistant professor who teaches technical and professional writing at New Mexico State University. They enjoy teaching students how to experiment with digital tools to effectively reach their audiences.

Katherine Mavridou-Hernandez is a graduate teaching associate and PhD candidate at the University of South Florida, where she teaches courses in first-year composition and technical and professional writing. Kat’s research focuses on digital rhetoric, the transfer of knowledge and practice across writing contexts, and the rhetoric of health and medicine.

Skye McMullen (they/them) is in San Francisco State University’s SF Scholars blended program, completing a BA in Professional Writing and Rhetoric and an MA in Composition. They served as Co-Editorin-Chief of the *Sutro Review* and worked as a communications and media assistant for the Institute for Civic and Community Engagement.

Burke Nixon teaches in the Program in Writing and Communication at Rice University in Houston, Texas.

Krystia Nora works at Milwaukee Area Technical College, has a PhD in English Composition Studies and TESOL from Indiana University of Pennsylvania. and she has published in *Ukrainian American Poets* *Respond, Writing on the Edge, CEA Forum*, and *Surviving Sexism in* *Academia*. Her scholarship explores composition studies, assessment, sociolinguistics, transnationalism, and disability studies.

J Palmeri (they / any) is Professor of English and Director of the Writing Program at Georgetown University

Enrique Paz is an assistant professor in the School of Literature, Writing, and Digital Humanities and the writing center director at Southern Illinois University Carbondale. His research explores how students develop their conceptions of writing and what kinds of experiences change those beliefs. Rich Rice is Professor of English in the Technical Communication & Rhetoric program at Texas Tech University where he teaches and publishes in grant writing, rhetoric, intercultural communication, new media, and online pedagogy. He directs TTU’s Center for Global Communication. For more information see https://richrice.com.

Megan Simmermeyer is currently pursuing her PhD in Rhetoric and Composition at the University of Pittsburgh. Her research lies with pedagogy, first-year writing, and the development of writerly voice. Megan holds a master’s in English from Northern Kentucky University and a BA in English from Mount St. Joseph University.

Kerry Smith-Saxman is a writing and rhetorical studies researcher with a background in composition pedagogy and technical communication. Her current research explores the intersections of affect theory and rhetorical theory. Her previous work has focused on teaching for transfer in pedagogy and curriculum in first-year writing.

Glen T. Southergill is Professor of Writing at Montana Technological University.

Mary K. Stewart is Associate Professor and the General Education Writing program coordinator at California State University San Marcos, where she teaches first-year writing and MA-level composition theory and pedagogy. Her research interests include hybrid and online writing instruction, writing program administration, and writing assessment.

Larry Sugisaki is Assistant Professor of Composition and Rhetoric at Methodist University where he teaches first-year and technical writing courses. His research interests include disability studies, writing center studies, and first-year writing.

Victoria Surtees is Teaching and Learning Specialist at the University of the Fraser Valley, in British Columbia Canada. She specializes in internationalization and culturally responsive teaching. Her research investigates international student perspectives on their teaching and learning experiences. Natalie E. Taylor, PhD, is Assistant Professor of English Composition at the University of Pittsburgh at Greensburg, where she teaches first-year writing and business writing.

Natalie E. Taylor, PhD, is Assistant Professor of English Composition at the University of Pittsburgh at Greensburg, where she teaches first-year writing and business writing.

Oguzhan Tekin is a teaching-stream assistant professor at the Institute for the Study of University Pedagogy, University of Toronto, Mississauga. He is a sociolinguist whose current research interests include sociolinguistics and writing for social change, critical language awareness, international students, students’ sense of belonging, and academic socialization in higher education.

Mariya Tseptsura is the director of the online writing program at the University of Arizona. Her research focuses on writing program administration, online writing instruction, and second language writing, and her work has appeared in multiple journals and edited collections. She has been teaching writing for over fifteen years.

Bethany Van Scooter is a writing studies scholar and lecturer at NC State whose work explores neurodivergence, generative AI, and inclusive pedagogy. She teaches first-year writing and professional writing and is pursuing a PhD in Communication, Rhetoric, and Digital Media.

An international student Vanshika, who landed in Canada in 2023, shares her lived experiences of racism here. Her words reflect the resilience and a commitment to foster respect, inclusion and a meaningful difference for international students.

Laura Vernon is a professor at Radford University where she teaches professional and business writing courses and produces scholarship in environmental rhetoric and in teaching and learning. Before earning her PhD in professional communication, she had a successful career as a newspaper editor, public relations practitioner, and environmental health writer.

Massimo Verzella is Associate Professor of English Composition at Penn State Behrend. His main areas of interests are in virtual exchange, translation, and international professional communication. As a member of Penn State’s EDGE (Experiential Digital Global Engagement), he promotes globally networked learning. Brandon Westfall is a graduate student in Composition and Rhetoric at the University of Central Oklahoma, where he also serves as Instructor of Record. As a queer Afrolatino first-generation college student, Brandon is passionate about advancing his academic career and exploring the intersections of culture and rhetoric. His research primarily focuses on cultural rhetorics, with particular interests in queer theory and rhetoric, digital rhetoric, gender theory and rhetoric, and race theory and rhetoric.

Lydia Wilkes is Assistant Professor of English and the writing program administrator at Auburn University. Her scholarship has appeared in *College Composition and Communication* and several edited collections. She co-edited *Rhetoric and Guns* and *Toward More Sustainable Metaphors of Writing Program Administration*. Her affiliations include the RhetAI Coalition.

David Williams is Assistant Professor of English at Spartanburg Methodist College. He earned his PhD in Rhetorics, Communication, and Information Design from Clemson University in 2025. His scholarly interests include film rhetoric, Holocaust representation, narrative studies, digital rhetoric, and composition pedagogy.
